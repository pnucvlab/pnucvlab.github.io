Research · Surgical & Embodied Vision

Seeing the operating room the way a surgical robot needs to.

Three perception modules that turn operating-room video into signals a robot can act on: where each instrument is, what the surgeon's hands are doing, and where bleeding occurs.

01 / Surgical tool recognition

Find, follow, and locate every instrument in 3D.

Starting from only a few labeled examples, the system learns to recognize surgical instruments, track them through the video, and estimate where each one sits in 3D space.

Few-shot

  1. Learn from a few examples

    A handful of examples per instrument is enough for the model to propose labels on new frames by itself, which people then review.

  2. Recognize and track

    Each instrument type, such as forceps or an electrosurgical pen, is identified and followed from frame to frame.

  3. Estimate 3D position and pose

    2D detections are combined with depth to recover each tool's 3D position and orientation.

02 / Hand pose recognition

Read the surgeon's hands in 3D.

The system locates 21 joints on every hand in view, lifts them into 3D, and works out which hand it is and whether it is open, so a robot knows when and where to hand over an instrument.

21 keypoints · 6-DoF

  1. 21 joints, in 3D

    Hands are detected in operating-room video, 21 joints are located, and depth gives each joint 3D coordinates plus the palm's full 6-DoF pose: position and tilt.

  2. Works without a depth camera

    When no depth sensor is available, depth is estimated from the regular camera image instead, so results stay consistent across equipment setups.

  3. Left or right, open or closed

    Even with several hands in view, each is labeled left or right and open or closed. The output goes directly to the robot controller and has been checked on real surgical footage.

03 / Bleeding detection & tracking

Spot bleeding and keep it in view.

A detection model and a tracking model work together so bleeding regions are found reliably and followed smoothly over time, even while the suction arm moves.

Real-time

  1. Detection plus tracking

    Combining the two models lets each cover the other's gaps, making results more stable and consistent from frame to frame.

  2. Live overlay for the surgeon

    The bleeding region is drawn on screen in real time to help the team stay focused on it.

  3. Linked to the suction arm

    The system works together with the suction arm, keeping the bleeding position steady even as the arm moves.
