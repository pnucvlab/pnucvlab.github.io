3DGS and 2DGS Scene Reconstruction and Relighting

Scene Reconstruction and Relighting with Three-Dimensional and Two-Dimensional Gaussian Splatting

Reconstructing surfaces.
Exploring light.

We study how images, depth estimation, normal estimation, and laser-scanned geometry can work together to reconstruct outdoor scenes. Our goal is to align Gaussian representations with scene surfaces and understand how geometry and materials shape their appearance under changing illumination.

  • Combine visual and geometric cues for scene reconstruction.
  • Refine surface structure through joint image, depth, and normal supervision.
  • Explore relighting and diagnose geometry-induced shadow artifacts.

Image observations guide visual appearance, while depth and surface orientation provide complementary constraints on the reconstructed geometry.

What to inspect
  • Road and building structure
  • Object boundaries and fine details
  • Differences from the captured view

Intermediate outputs reveal surface structure beyond the final image. Colors in the normal map represent orientation, not material color.

02 / Surface structure

Look beneath
the rendered image.

Depth estimation, normal estimation, and laser-scanned geometry inform our research on surface-consistent reconstruction. Rendered geometry helps reveal where the representation remains inconsistent.

What to inspect
  • Surface continuity and depth boundaries
  • Orientation of roads and building facades
  • Geometric artifacts hidden by appearance

Recorded relighting of the reconstructed scene.

03 / Lighting & shadows

Study how light
reveals geometry.

Relighting makes the relationship between geometry, materials, and visibility easier to inspect. We investigate how surface errors produce irregular shadows and how reconstruction can better support lighting changes.

What to inspect
  • Shading across roads and objects
  • Shadow shape and surface alignment
  • Remaining visibility and surface artifacts

These are recorded research examples, not a live renderer or a controlled comparison between Gaussian representations. Surface and shadow artifacts remain under investigation.
